# Lane: sky (sky and time of day, battle-scarred ground, weapon trails)

All of it is cosmetic and local: every machine computes it from what it already has (the stage number and clock, the synced bullets and enemies, effects-bus events). No codec change and no new effect types; chain zaps now send one burst per hop (with an `arc` field) instead of four, so the effects stream got smaller.

## What players see
- **Sunsets that progress.** Each stage goes midday -> golden hour (the sun turns gold from about a third of the way in) -> sunset (the last stretch and the boss: orange-pink sun, violet shadows, pink fog, a warm sunset colour grade). Every biome has its own palette (Meadow pink-orange, Dunes deep orange, Peaks rose and lilac, Magma red and wine).
- **Sky light.** The far edge of the view glows with the sky toward the sun (pale blue haze at noon, gold, then sunset orange with a soft sun glow up-screen; a cool blue haze at night). Screen-blended, so it brightens and tints but never muddies.
- **Cloud shadows.** Soft cloud shadows drift across the field and everything on it (ground, walls, enemies, heroes), projected along the sun so they slide with a low sun. More cover and darker on rainy stages, fainter moonlit ones at night, ash clouds on Magma Isle.
- **Sun shafts and moonbeams.** At low sun, slanted golden light shafts drift across the field; on night stages faint silver moonbeams.
- **The sky in the water.** The sea (Meadow, Dunes, and an icy sheen on Peaks; never the lava sea) and the ponds mirror the sky: its gradient, the drifting clouds, sun glitter on the waves, and on night stages twinkling stars, the moon and a moon path of glints. The top-down camera never sees the horizon, so this (plus the shafts and the sky light) is how the sky dome shows.
- **Battle-scarred ground.** Big explosions leave craters (a dark bowl with a lit rim and sun-facing relief, cracked inside), scorch marks, and frost novas freeze patches of ground (pale ice with glints). On Magma Isle craters crack open into glowing lava that cools from white-hot to red to dark cracks over ~20 s. Everything stays for most of the stage, so the field carries its battle history; marks on the same spot merge (never stack darker), and when too much of the field is scarred the oldest (faintest) marks fade faster, so heavy explosive builds stay readable. The old 10-30 s dark blob scars for scorch/crater/frost are gone; goo splats and boss fire still use short splats.
- **Ribbon trails.** Discs, missiles, bounce drones and charge shots leave glowing ribbons that taper away (they replace the old box streaks). Big shots (charge shots, Lance Shot, Mega Barrage, plasma) leave ribbons too. The Rail Driver leaves a corkscrew spiral round its line plus a lingering vapour line.
- **Every projectile has an identity.** Every bullet gets a soft tail that grows out of the gun. Enemy shots throb (easier to read as danger). Fire shots (Flare Buster, Dragon's Breath, Star Cannon, mega shots, Scorch Turret) leave flickering flame ribbons (yellow -> orange -> red) and shimmer the air with heat haze. Volt Buster bolts crackle with little arcs. Pellets and turret rounds are long thin tracers.
- **Lightning.** Chain Zap draws thick jagged bolts with side branches and a soft glow, re-rolled 30 times a second so they flicker; chain zaps (Storm Crown, Static relic, Storm Rail, Thunderstorm, pet) are real arcs instead of dotted lines; shocked enemies crackle with small arcs.

## Settings respected
- QUALITY: LOW turns off trails (like the old streaks; lightning falls back to the thin lines, chain zaps to the dotted line), sun shafts, star and glitter detail. Cloud shadows, reflections, scars and the sky light run on every rung (cheap).
- REDUCE FLASHING: electric arcs stop flickering and re-roll their shape at a third of the rate.
- SHOCKWAVES (distortion) / SCREEN SHAKE 0: no heat haze over fire shots.
- NIGHT STAGES: night sky (stars, moon, moonbeams) follows the existing night factor.

## Key numbers (debug panel folder "Sky, scars & trails")
- `skyTuning` (src/render/sky.ts): cloud tile 72 m, wind 1.5 / 0.6 m/s, strength 0.55 (multiplies the sun term), cut 0.54, soft 0.16, height 30 m, rain +0.2 cover; reflection 0.6 (x biome: meadow/dunes 1, peaks 0.45, magma 0; x1.45 at sunset); 7 shafts at 0.32 from time of day 0.3; moonbeams 0.14; sky light 0.08 day / 0.34 sunset / 0.14 night.
- `sunsetWeights(k)`: gold = smoothstep(0.08, 0.55, k), dusk = smoothstep(0.5, 1, k); `k` is the existing `game.dayK` (0 until 35% of the stage, 1 at the boss).
- `scarTuning` (src/render/groundScars.ts): 512x512 map over +-60 m (0.23 m a texel), scorch alpha 0.5, crater 0.6; fade per second scorch 1/260, crater 1/300, heat 1/5 (Magma 1/22), frost 1/45; sweep 2 s; budget 10% of the map scarred, up to 8x faster fading at 2x the budget.
- `trailTuning` (src/game/trails.ts): up to 4096 live segments, arcs 30/s, branch chance 0.3. `bulletFxTuning` (bullets.ts): per frame at most 64 fire ribbons, 14 crackles, 40 big-shot ribbons; enemy throb 12%. `skyFxTuning` (systems/sky.ts): heat haze 0.7 (merged within 3 m), 6 crackles per re-roll. `MAX_HEAT` 4 -> 8 (lava plus fire shots).
- Cost, heavy bench scene (`npm run bench`, HIGH, headless SwiftShader, 15 s): before the lane 9.3 fps / CPU per 60 fps frame 1.51 ms / 169 draw calls; after 9.6 fps / 1.58 ms / 170-173 draw calls. `ren.sky` 0.02-0.03 ms a frame. New draws: sky light, sea reflection, ponds, shafts, scar overlay, trails, bullet tails (the old streak and most of the scar splat pool are gone). A first version drew the whole 4096-slot trail ring every frame and cost a third of the frame in software GL; trails are now a packed pool and only live segments are drawn.

## How it works (for maintainers)
- `src/render/sky.ts`: palettes and pure `timeOfDay` / `sunsetWeights` / `skySunDir` / `cloudNoise`; `SkyFx` owns the reflections (a transparent overlay just above the sea and instanced discs over water patches from `ambience.surfaces.patches`), the shafts (camera-facing instanced quads) and the sky light (full-screen triangle, screen blend). The cloud map is bound to every retro material through `retroGlobals.uCloudMap/uCloud/uCloudCut`.
- `src/render/biomeLook.ts`: `applyTimeOfDay` now takes its day/sunset colours from `timeOfDay` and blends `SUNSET_GRADE` into the area grade (small edit; the night blend is unchanged).
- `src/render/retroMaterial.ts`: three cloud uniforms and five shader lines that scale the sun term (unlit things are untouched).
- `src/render/groundScars.ts`: `ScarMap` (pure: stamp with max, fade sweep, coverage budget, dirty row spans) and `GroundScars` (DataTexture uploaded by row ranges, one overlay mesh with crater relief from the map gradient, lava cracks, frost glints, cloud shadows and fog). Its overlays leave the bloom mask (alpha) alone.
- `src/game/scars.ts`: `add()` routes scorch/crater/frost colours (`scarKind`) to the map; other colours stay short splats. Callers (`vfx.scar`) and the 'g' effect event are unchanged.
- `src/game/trails.ts`: camera-facing segments (styles Glow, Fire, Electric, Soft), faded in the shader against a clock that stops while the game is frozen; `arc()` builds jagged, branching bolts (`arcPath` is pure). `trails.flush()` runs again at the end of render so pieces added by weapons after the clock ticked show the same frame. `src/game/streaks.ts` is now a thin wrapper over it (same API).
- `src/game/bullets.ts`: `bulletLook(team, colour, width, length)` (pure, works on clients) picks the look at spawn; render draws tails (one instanced mesh sharing the colour buffer) and adds the capped trail pieces. `render(alpha, dt)` now takes the frame dt (render.ts passes `rdt`).
- `src/game/systems/sky.ts` (`updateSkyFx`, called in render() after the look pass): drives SkyFx, the Magma flag on the scar map, heat haze spots over fire shots, shock crackle, the `arc` bursts (it wraps `particles.burst` once, like the look pass wraps `fx.ring`) and the late trail flush.

## New test-link options
- `&tod=<0..1>`: pin the time of day (0 midday, 0.5 golden hour, 1 sunset).
- `&scars=<n>[:crater|scorch|frost]`: n scars around the hero after 0.5 s (mixed kinds by default).
- `&clouds=<0..1>`: cloud shadow strength (0 = off, handy for scar close-ups).
- Examples: `?test&stage=2&tod=0.6&quality=high` (golden dunes), `?test&stage=1&tod=0.9&zoom=2.2&at=10,-52` (sunset sea), `?test&stage=2&night&zoom=2.2&at=10,-52` (moon on the sea), `?test&stage=4&scars=12:crater&frozen` (lava cracks), `?test&stage=1&weapons=lightning:6&hold=60` (arcs), `?test&stage=1&loadout=rail,charge,bomb&aim=1,0&swarm=60` (rail spiral).

## Unfinished or risky
- **No visible sky dome.** The fixed 3/4 camera (58 deg pitch, 34 deg FOV) never sees above the horizon, even zoomed out, so a dome mesh would never be drawn. The sky model exists (palettes, sun, moon, stars) and shows through reflections, shafts, the sky light, cloud shadows and the light itself. If a future camera (title, cinematic) looks at the horizon, a dome can reuse `SKY_PALETTES` and the reflection shader's gradient/stars code.
- **The sky sun and the light sun differ on purpose.** Lighting keeps its sun behind the camera (faces stay lit); the sky's visual sun sits up-screen so its glow, glitter and shafts are visible. Shadows of clouds follow the lighting sun.
- **Sea reflections are only visible near the island's edge** (or zoomed out), as the sea only shows there. Ponds show them everywhere.
- **Scar map resolution** is fixed at 512 (0.23 m a texel), so marks are deliberately chunky pixel art; arenas larger than +-60 m would need `scarTuning.extent` raised (the map is allocated once).
- **Haze is drawn after the opaque world** but before transparent effects, so particles, scars and trails aren't tinted by it.
- **Trail segments are per frame**, so at very low frame rates ribbons look dashed; at 60 fps they read as continuous.
- Monkey-patch: systems/sky.ts wraps `particles.burst` at runtime (as look.ts does with `fx.ring`). If BurstOptions changes shape, arcs silently stop (typecheck catches signature changes).
- Measured in headless Chromium (SwiftShader) only; not on a real iGPU. The new fill is small (thin trails, one full-screen triangle, the scar overlay early-outs on empty texels).
